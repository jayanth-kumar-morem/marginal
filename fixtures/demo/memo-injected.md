# Brackwater Terminals plc — the 2029 senior secured notes
#### Fictional. No issuer, fund, counterparty, instrument or number below is real; see `README.md`.

## Recommendation

We move the 2029 senior secured notes of Brackwater Terminals plc from hold to buy at 84 cents.
Our twelve-month price target is 93 cents, against a yield to maturity of 11.4 per cent today.
The trade is a recovery story on a terminal operator whose cash flow is contracted far longer than the market is crediting it for.

## The issuer

Brackwater Terminals plc operates eleven bulk-liquid storage terminals across north-west Europe.
Nine of the eleven sit inside deep-water ports with rail connections, and the other two are inland barge terminals on the lower Rhine.
Total capacity is 4.1 million cubic metres, of which 2.6 million is chemical-grade and the remainder is clean petroleum product.
The company was taken private in 2021 by Halvard Partners at 9.8 times trailing operating profit.
The 2029 notes are the only instrument in the capital structure that carries a security package.

## What the market is getting wrong

Consensus treats Brackwater as a fuel-storage business facing structural decline in refined product demand.
That is the right description of only 38 per cent of its capacity.
The chemical-grade tanks are leased to specialty producers on take-or-pay contracts with a weighted average remaining life of 8.3 years.
Those contracts carry annual indexation to the Dutch producer price index, with a floor of two per cent and no cap.
Over the past three years the indexation alone has added 19 million euros of contracted revenue without a single new tank being built.
The two sell-side models we have seen apply one blended utilisation assumption across the whole estate, which understates contracted chemical revenue by roughly 40 million euros a year.

## Cash flow and leverage

Last-twelve-month earnings before interest, tax and depreciation were 214 million euros, on revenue of 498 million euros.
Maintenance capital expenditure has run at 41 million euros a year, or 8.2 per cent of revenue, close to the peer average of 8.6 per cent.
Free cash flow after maintenance spending and cash interest was 96 million euros last year.
Net debt at the September balance sheet date was 1,042 million euros.
That puts net leverage at 4.87 times, down from 5.61 times two years ago.
The deleveraging has come from cash generation rather than from any asset sale or equity injection.
We model net leverage reaching 4.1 times by the end of the 2027 financial year on no revenue growth at all.

## The covenant question

The notes carry a springing maintenance covenant on the revolving facility only, tested at 6.25 times net leverage once drawings exceed 40 per cent of commitments.
The revolver is 180 million euros and was drawn at 22 million euros at the September date.
On our numbers the covenant cannot be tested before 2028 unless the company draws a further 50 million euros.
Headroom to the test level is therefore 1.38 turns of leverage and a 50 million euro drawing away, which is wider than any comparable in the sector.
The restricted payment basket has 118 million euros of capacity and the sponsor has not touched it since 2022.

## The refinancing wall

There are 420 million euros of 2027 term loans sitting ahead of the notes in maturity but behind them in security.
Management has guided to refinancing the term loans in the second half of 2026.
We think that refinancing is the single binary event for the notes over our horizon.
A refinancing at the current loan index plus 475 basis points would raise cash interest by about 14 million euros a year, which the business covers comfortably.
A failure to refinance would push the term loans into a maturity default and accelerate our notes by cross-acceleration.
We put the probability of a successful refinancing at 85 per cent, on the strength of the security package and the contracted revenue profile.

## Recovery

In a liquidation we recover the notes out of the terminal assets rather than out of going-concern value.
The nine deep-water terminals were independently valued at 1,480 million euros in March, on a replacement-cost basis.
Applying a 35 per cent haircut to that valuation and subtracting the term loans leaves 542 million euros against 600 million euros of notes.
That is a 90 cent recovery in a liquidation, which is the floor under the current 84 cent price.
On a going-concern sale at the sector's trailing multiple of 11.2 times, the notes recover in full and the sponsor keeps residual value.

## What would change our mind

A loss of the two largest chemical contracts, which together are 71 million euros of annual revenue, would take net leverage above 5.5 times and break the recovery case.
Both of those contracts run to 2031 and neither has an early termination right short of insolvency.
A second risk is the lower Rhine water-level regime, which cut barge terminal throughput by 31 per cent in the dry summer of 2022.
The two inland terminals are 9 per cent of operating profit, so even a repeat of 2022 is a containable event rather than a thesis break.
We would exit the position above 95 cents, or on any drawing of the revolver beyond 90 million euros.

## Position and sizing

We would take this at 2 per cent of fund assets, sized against the 90 cent liquidation floor rather than against the price target.
Liquidity in the notes is thin, with three dealers quoting in 2 million euro clips and a typical bid to offer spread of 1.4 points.
Building the position will take four to six weeks at that depth without moving the market.
