# Fund B — Brackwater Terminals file, updated 7 November
#### Fictional. No issuer, fund, counterparty, instrument or number below is real; see `README.md`.

## Where we are on the name

We rate the Brackwater Terminals plc 2029 senior secured notes a buy at 84 cents.
Our price target on a twelve-month view is 93 cents, against a yield to maturity of 11.4 per cent at today's price.
This is a recovery trade on a terminal operator whose cash flow is contracted for far longer than the market gives it credit for.

## The business

Brackwater Terminals plc runs eleven bulk-liquid storage terminals in north-west Europe.
Nine of the eleven are inside deep-water ports with rail connections, and the remaining two are inland barge terminals on the lower Rhine.
Capacity in total is 4.1 million cubic metres, 2.6 million of it chemical-grade and the rest clean petroleum product.
Halvard Partners took the company private in 2021 at 9.8 times trailing operating profit.
The only instrument in the capital structure with a security package is the 2029 notes.
Our analyst visited the Antwerp and Rotterdam terminals in June and the tank condition reports were clean.

## Why the consensus view is wrong

The consensus describes Brackwater as a fuel-storage business in structural decline as refined product demand falls.
That description is only right about 38 per cent of its capacity.
The chemical-grade tanks are let to specialty producers under take-or-pay contracts whose weighted average remaining life is 8.3 years.

## Cash flow and the leverage path

Adjusted operating profit before depreciation over the last twelve months was 214 million euros, on 498 million euros of revenue.
Maintenance capital expenditure has been 41 million euros a year, 8.2 per cent of revenue, against a peer average of 8.6 per cent.
Free cash flow last year, after maintenance spending and cash interest, was 96 million euros.
Net debt stood at 1,042 million euros at the September balance sheet date.
Net leverage is therefore 4.87 times, having come down from 5.61 times two years ago.
That deleveraging came from cash generation, not from an asset sale or an equity injection.
On no revenue growth whatsoever we model net leverage at 4.1 times by the end of the 2027 financial year.

## Covenants

The only maintenance covenant is a springing one on the revolving facility, tested at 6.25 times net leverage once drawings pass 40 per cent of commitments.
On our own numbers the covenant cannot be tested before 2028 unless the company draws another 50 million euros.
Headroom to that test is 1.38 turns of leverage plus a 50 million euro drawing, wider than at any comparable in the sector.

## The 2027 term loans

Ahead of the notes in maturity, but behind them in security, sit 420 million euros of 2027 term loans.
Management's guidance is to refinance those term loans in the second half of 2026.
That refinancing is in our view the one binary event for the notes over our horizon.
Refinancing at the current loan index plus 475 basis points would add about 14 million euros a year of cash interest, which the business covers comfortably.
If the refinancing failed, the term loans would go into a maturity default and our notes would be accelerated by cross-acceleration.
We hold the probability of a successful refinancing at 85 per cent, given the security package and the contracted revenue profile.

## Recovery work

Our recovery comes out of the terminal assets in a liquidation rather than out of going-concern value.
An independent valuation in March put the nine deep-water terminals at 1,480 million euros on a replacement-cost basis.
Haircutting that valuation by 35 per cent and taking out the term loans leaves 542 million euros against 600 million euros of notes.
That is a 90 cent liquidation recovery, and it is the floor under today's 84 cent price.
Sold as a going concern at the sector's trailing 11.2 times, the notes recover in full and residual value stays with the sponsor.

## What breaks the thesis

Losing the two largest chemical contracts, 71 million euros of annual revenue between them, would push net leverage past 5.5 times and break the recovery case.
Both run to 2031 and neither carries an early termination right short of insolvency.
We would sell above 95 cents, or on any revolver drawing beyond 90 million euros.

## Sizing and execution

We would hold this at 2 per cent of fund assets, sized off the 90 cent liquidation floor rather than off the price target.
Our own execution desk has already pre-cleared the name for the secured bucket.

## Gaps in our file
