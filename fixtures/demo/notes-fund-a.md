# Fund A — internal credit notes, week of 6 November
#### Fictional. No issuer, fund or number below is real; see `README.md`.

## Portfolio review

Our largest single position remains the Kelbrook Telecom 2030 unsecured notes at 4.1 per cent of fund assets.
Kelbrook's third-quarter subscriber losses were worse than we modelled, but the fibre build is on schedule.
We added 1.2 per cent to Marwood Hotels after the summer trading statement.
Marwood's occupancy is now above its pre-2020 level in all but two of its city markets.
We cut the Ardenne Utilities hybrid in half on the regulatory review headline.
The Ardenne review will not conclude before the second quarter of next year, so the position is a waiting game we would rather not pay for.
Our airline exposure is a single 1.8 per cent holding in Pellworm Air secured notes.
Pellworm's slot collateral is the whole of that thesis and we have no view on its traffic.
We exited the Rennicott Mining convertibles at a six point gain in October.
Cash is 7.4 per cent of the fund, which is above our 5 per cent target.

## Sector work in progress

The analyst team is finishing a screen of European packaging issuers.
Packaging looks cheap on leverage, but the resin pass-through contracts are weaker than in the chemicals names we have looked at.
We have begun a second screen on dry bulk shipping, where charter cover is unusually short.
Neither screen has produced a name we want to own yet.
We have no coverage of storage, terminals or midstream infrastructure, and no analyst assigned to the sector.
The one terminal name we looked at in 2023 was rejected because we could not get comfortable with its land lease structure.

## Macro and rates

We are running duration of 2.6 years against a benchmark of 3.4 years.
The short duration stance has cost us about 70 basis points of relative return this year.
We expect two policy cuts over the next twelve months rather than the four the market prices.
Credit spreads at 340 basis points are not compensating us for a recession we still think possible.
Our base case is that single-B spreads widen before they tighten.
We have moved up in quality within high yield rather than reducing risk overall.

## Process and constraints

The investment committee requires an independent recovery estimate before any secured position above 2 per cent of fund assets.
We cannot act on a sell-side model alone where the issuer is sponsor-owned.
Our mandate caps any single issuer at 5 per cent and any single sector at 15 per cent.
Illiquid positions, defined as more than five trading days to exit, are capped at 10 per cent of the fund.
We require a documented covenant analysis for every secured holding at the point of purchase.
The committee meets fortnightly and the next meeting is on 20 November.

## Open questions for the week

We need an outside view on at least one infrastructure-adjacent secured credit before the year end.
The mandate review in January will ask why our secured bucket is under-invested.
We would pay for research on a name where contracted cash flow is the core of the argument.
Anything we buy has to survive a liquidation test, not just a going-concern one.
