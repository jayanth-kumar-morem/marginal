## Hard technical facts that constrain the design

1. **FHE is ~1,000–10,000× too slow for LLM appraisal.** Llama-3-8B under CKKS needs **18 s per generated token and 20 s per 128-token prefill on 8 high-end GPUs** (2601.18511, the best published result). Prior SOTA was 295 s on H100. Implication: TEEs are the only practical substrate for running a buyer's LLM over a seller's data. FHE fits only tiny scoring kernels (cf. 2512.06033, which needed low-rank projections even on GPT-2).
2. **zkML tops out around 10 minutes per 8–13B inference, and training is per step.** zkLLM: 13B in under 15 min. zkFinGPT: Llama3-8B at 531 s commit + 620 s prove. ZK-LoRA fine-tuning: 17–31 s **per training step** (2510.16830). Implication: ZK proves (a) small appraisal sub-models (Jolt Atlas), (b) one final verdict, or (c) TOPLOC-style cheap commitments. It cannot be the per-query trust layer.
3. **TEE overhead is low only when tuned, and only Blackwell protects multi-GPU.** B200 + TDX costs 1–3% tuned, but **30–40% on stock stacks** (2608.26575). H100 costs 4–8% but has no NVLink or HBM protection (2509.18886). Cold model loads under CC cost 45–70% throughput (2505.16501). Implication: Blackwell for multi-GPU appraisal, warm per-buyer enclaves, and a dedicated perf-engineering line item.
4. **Physical attacks on CPU TEEs cost $50–$1,000 and vendors call them out of scope.** Battering RAM (<$50, DDR4), WireTap (DDR4, SGX attestation key), TEE.fail (<$1k, DDR5, TDX/SEV-SNP plus NVIDIA attestation forgery). Root cause: deterministic AES-XTS without freshness. Implication: the operator who physically hosts the box must be trusted or diversified. Use multi-vendor or multi-operator attestation (k-of-n enclaves on different providers), cap deal value per enclave, and add insurance (cf. 2512.08737). GPU-CC also leaks metadata on the bounce-buffer path (2507.02770).
5. **Weight-level unlearning cannot back a "forget certificate."** 4-bit quantization restores **83%** of "unlearned" knowledge (2410.16454), and minimal fine-tuning reverses unlearning (2505.16831). Implication: the certificate must attest that seller data never left volatile enclave memory. That means no persistence, enclave teardown, and destruction of the sealing key. Pair it with dataset-inference deterrence (2507.03619) as the post-hoc enforcement layer.
6. **Shapley and LOO valuations are not truthful prices.** Myerson payments are the minimal truthful, buyer-optimal rule (2504.05563). Data value is heavy-tailed, with most items negative for capability (2512.06033). Implication: use valuation for allocation and selection, use a mechanism (Myerson/VCG or near-efficient FPA when sellers are substitutable, 2610.20587) for payment, and price per item rather than flat.
7. **LLM agents are biased, manipulable negotiators.** Magentic Marketplace shows a **10–30× advantage for speed over quality** (first-proposal bias), with welfare collapsing at scale (2510.25779). AgenticPay shows large buyer/seller asymmetries (2602.06008). Information design via LLM framing is optimizable by the seller (2509.25565). Implication: agents should fill structured bids into a fixed mechanism, not haggle in free text. Run batched or sealed rounds, and keep appraiser outputs numeric and structured.
8. **Agent-payment protocols are not atomic or context-bound.** x402 has 5 practical attacks (paid-but-denied, unpaid-service) (2605.11781). AP2 mandates don't capture manipulated pre-authorization context (2608.23858). There are 40 new formal-consistency findings across x402/MPP/ACP/AP2 (2609.00060). Implication: settlement must be atomic DvP. The escrow TEE releases the decryption key and the NEAR Intent settles in one attested step bound to the appraisal hash.
9. **Shared caches leak across tenants, and split-TEE obfuscation is broken.** KV/prefix caching enables ~95–99% prompt reconstruction (2508.08438). Partial TEE-shielding with static masks recovers Llama-3-8B layer secrets in ~6 min (2602.11088). Implication: use strict per-deal isolation (no cross-deal KV cache) and full-GPU-CC only.
