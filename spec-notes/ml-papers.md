# ML papers: verification notes for SPEC.md

Compiled 2026-11-11 for the Marginal spec. Scope: the checklist papers as they are used in `MOONSHOT.md` §3, §4.3, §4.4 #4 and §4.6 T4–T5, and in `moonshot-notes/papers.md` §B–H, plus new literature for the appraisers and the threat model.

**Method**
- **Metadata.** IDs, titles, first authors and dates come from three arXiv export-API calls:
  - batch 1: 39 checklist IDs;
  - batch 2: 32 new candidate IDs;
  - batch 3: 2 IDs found after batch 2.
- All 73 IDs resolved, and every title matched the paper it was cited for. I read all 73 abstracts.
- **Full text.** I opened `arxiv.org/html/ID` only where MOONSHOT or papers.md relies on a number that is not in the abstract, or where the abstract was too thin to judge a claim. That was 15 papers: 2606.05165, 2601.15716, 2510.16830, 2506.23706, 2508.21393, 2510.18554, 2508.19843, 2510.18109, 2509.26598, 2507.03619, 2406.06443, 2506.06975, 2603.05786, 2405.13954 and 2410.16454.
- **Source labels.** PRIMARY = arXiv abstract or paper text, or an official venue or project page. SECONDARY = a search-engine summary or a third-party page. UNVERIFIED = could not be checked.
- **Where a number was read.** **abs** = arXiv listing abstract (export API). **full** = paper body (arxiv.org/html). **snip** = search snippet (SECONDARY).
- Unless marked otherwise, every arXiv source is PRIMARY at `https://arxiv.org/abs/<ID>` for abs and `https://arxiv.org/html/<ID>` for full.

### 1b. Flags for SPEC.md (overstatements and missing caveats)

1. **STRIDE "13×"** (§3.1 row 3; papers.md #31): use "~12× vs AirRep and 5× vs LoGRA at 1.38B params (baseline runtimes extrapolated), one H100" ([2606.05165](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05165), full).
2. **"Cheap proxies (in-run Shapley, STRIDE)"** (§10 P2): neither is a pre-screen.
   - In-Run Data Shapley needs the actual training run ([2406.11011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11011)).
   - STRIDE took 9.9 h on an H100 for a 1.38B model, while the GTE-embedding baseline took 2.99 h for about 77% of its LDS ([2606.05165](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05165), Table 1; the ratio is my calculation).
3. **Attestable Audits "TDX/SEV-SNP/Nitro"** (§3.1 row 5): the prototype is Nitro-only, CPU, 4-bit 8B, about 100× slower than a GPU ([2506.23706](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23706)). It shows feasibility, not that GPU-scale confidential evaluation is "proven practical".
4. **ManaTEE "signs eval results with model and script hashes"** (§3.1 row 5): UNVERIFIED. The blog was unreachable, and the README and site don't say this; the README calls the project "not production-ready". Soften the wording or drop it.
5. **VFT/VeriLoRA "ZK proofs that a LoRA was trained only on a committed dataset … NDSS'26"** (§3.1 row 6):
   - VeriLoRA (NDSS'26) proves step correctness at about 1000× training cost (about 2–4 min per single-sample step on an A100), with no dataset binding found.
   - The dataset-binding claim, and the "17–31 s per step" figure (papers.md hard fact 2), come from VFT, which names no base model, no proof system and no code.
6. **Dataset-inference deterrent** (§3.2 #3, §4.4 #4):
   - papers.md misattributes the "MIA ≈ random" result.
   - The deterrent works only for datasets; it needs seller-trained reference models ([2507.03619](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03619)) or an IID held-out split plus loss access ([2406.06443](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06443)).
   - It does not apply to a normal no-deal, because that buyer never held plaintext. Reword as "deters misuse after a breach or of copy-mode deliveries".
7. **"Restores 83%"** (§3.2 #3): the paper says "retains on average 83% after 4-bit quantization (vs 21% in full precision), for utility-constrained unlearning, on MUSE" ([2410.16454](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16454)).
8. **Leakage budget "no prior implementation / nothing like it in the papers stream"** (§4.4 #3, §9.3): cite the Ladder ([1502.04585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)) and Dwork et al. ([1506.02629](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629)) as the theory, and claim novelty only for the priced, on-chain enforcement.
9. **T2 "seller prompt injection: residual Low"** (§4.6): Low is defensible only if every LLM output is a downward-only multiplier.
   - The literature shows gradient-optimised attacks ([2403.17710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17710)), one-token "master keys" that fool GPT-o1 and Claude-4 judges ([2507.08794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08794)), and universal phrases that transfer across judges ([2402.14016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14016)).
   - Transfer weakens the "dual-model min()" defence.
   - The appraiser's codehash is public for attestation, so if the model is open-weight, sellers get white-box access to it: the strongest JudgeDeceiver setting (my analysis).
   - The "deterministic" novelty score sits downstream of LLM claim extraction (as in SAFE, [2403.18802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)), which is a second injection surface (my analysis).
10. **Knowledge appraiser "verifiability (cross-check against public sources)"** (§4.3): SAFE verifies each fact with Google Search queries ([2403.18802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)). Running that from inside a room would send queries derived from the sealed claims to a third party. Use an in-room snapshot of public corpora, or PIR, instead (my analysis).
11. **Prior art to add to §9:**
    - NovAScore ([2409.09249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09249)) already scores document novelty as an aggregate of atomic-fact novelty and salience against historical documents. That is the core of the knowledge appraiser, minus the buyer-specific corpus and the TEE.
    - "Information potential" ([2502.13691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13691)) already measures a collection's information gain for an LLM without training.
12. **Precision fixes:**
    - AP2 "8 High" means High in at least one of five architectures ([2608.23858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23858)).
    - Tamarin "40 findings" are formal-consistency findings, of which 10 were validated ([2609.00060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00060)).
    - Spruce's NDCG figures are relative ([2609.03376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03376)).
    - The ≥10× figure from 2510.18554 is measured only on OLMo 2 and Open-Reasoner-Zero ([2510.18554](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18554)).

### 2h. Implications for SPEC.md (my analysis, based on the sources above)

- **Dataset appraiser, a staged pipeline from cheapest to most expensive.**
  1. Embedding overlap and semantic dedupe (2303.09540). STRIDE's own table shows a GTE-embedding baseline reaching about 77% of STRIDE's LDS in about 30% of the time (2606.05165).
  2. Forward-pass-only proxies using the buyer's model: 2505.12212, 2407.12259, 2502.13691.
  3. Gradient features: 2402.04333, 2405.13954.
  4. A LoRA lift test, with rectified extrapolation (2402.02314), a stated confidence about 80% from DataDecide (2504.11393), and In-Run Shapley computed during the lift test (2406.11011).
  - Show results as ranges, because "no single method dominates" (2507.09424).
- **Knowledge appraiser.**
  - Extract claims with two different extractors and union the results (2502.10855, 2406.19276).
  - Compute novelty by semantic retrieval over the buyer corpus (2510.27313, 2303.13408). Benchmark against NovAScore (2409.09249).
  - Verify against an offline snapshot of public corpora, not live search (2403.18802).
  - Treat the extractor as an injection surface too (2403.17710, 2410.05451).
- **Eval-set listings.**
  - `contamination_check` = set-level tests only (2310.17623, 2308.08493), never instance-level MIA flags (2402.07841, 2410.18966).
  - Meter repeated `score` queries with Ladder-style rules (1502.04585).
- **Model listings.**
  - Use white-box static fingerprints for lineage (2508.19843), but say their adversarial robustness is untested (2509.26598).
  - Weights-in-vault APIs should hide logprobs (2403.06634).
- **Forget certificate.** Keep the "non-persistence, not unlearning" framing (2410.16454, 2505.16831). Narrow the dataset-inference deterrent to dataset listings, and require sellers to keep an IID held-out split (2406.06443, 2507.03619).
- **ZK.** Present ZK training proofs as rare post-deal audits (VeriLoRA costs about 1000× training compute; 2508.21393). Do not cite VFT numbers until its model and code are public (2510.16830).
